Built so your family's life stays your family's.

Most family apps keep your family's life on someone else's servers, in a format only they can use, and pay for it with a subscription or with your data. Yggltree works the other way round: it runs on your own computer and phones, the code is open for anyone to read, and nobody sits in the middle.

Your data, your control.

It lives on your family's computer and phones. Back it up, open the folder, and take it with you. Nobody else holds the keys.

Nobody in the middle.

No account with us, no ads, no tracking, and nothing sold. We run no servers, so we never see your data. The relay is on your own Cloudflare account and only ever holds scrambled data.

Room for what matters.

A calendar, a shopping list and a chore chart should help a family spend time together, not become another feed. There is no feed to scroll, and notifications only say that something changed.

Software you own.

Free and open source under the MIT License. Read it, change it, share it. It doesn't depend on us staying around.

Everything the household runs on, in one place.

See it in action.

Real screens from the app, from a made-up family. Browse them with the arrows or the dots.

  1. A shopping list the whole family can add to.

    Anyone can add an item from their phone, even from the car park. Each item keeps its store and category, so the list reads the way you shop.

  2. One calendar for everyone.

    Add an event with a time, assign it to the person it belongs to, and everyone sees it on their own phone.

  3. Chores and allowance that add up.

    Assign a chore with a reward. When it's finished, the reward goes into that person's balance, so the allowance keeps itself honest.

  4. Recipes that become a shopping list.

    Save a recipe with its ingredients, then add them all to the shopping list in one tap. Take the items off again the same way.

  5. It works without signal, and catches up later.

    Phones keep working with no connection. Anything you change is saved on the phone and syncs once it reaches the family relay again, on Wi-Fi or mobile data.

Built to grow, one module at a time.

Yggltree is a platform, not a fixed app. Every tool is a module on the same base, so new tools and better versions of the ones you have arrive over time.

Every tool is open to everyone.

Nothing to switch on. On a phone, each person pins up to four tools to their bottom menu, in the order they want, and the pins follow them to every device.

New modules share your family.

Every tool uses the same family members, colors and sync. A new module works with the calendar, to-dos and chores you already use.

Updates reach you.

When a new version is ready, the app tells you, so you can keep every tool current.

Private by design, in plain words.

Your family's information lives on your family's devices. To sync phones, the app passes it through a small relay on your own Cloudflare account. The relay only ever holds scrambled data. The key that unscrambles it is in the family link you share with your phones, so the relay can't read any of it.

Three steps, then it's just your family.

  1. 1

    Install on the family computer.

    Set up your relay.

    Scan the code on each phone.

For the curious.

How the sync and the privacy work, for people who want the details.

  • Changes merge, they don't overwrite. Each device edits its own copy, and the copies are merged with Yjs CRDTs, so two people editing offline end up with the same result.
  • Scrambled before it leaves the phone. Data is encrypted with XChaCha20-Poly1305. The keys are derived from the family key with HKDF-SHA256.
  • A small relay, on your own account. The relay runs on Cloudflare Workers with Durable Objects and stores only encrypted updates. Deleting it deletes its stored data.
  • Read the code. The app, the family computer's server, and the relay are all in the repository, with developer notes in Yggltree_vault.

Questions people ask first.

Is it free?

The software is free and MIT licensed. The relay runs on your own Cloudflare account, and Cloudflare's free plan covers a family's use. Its limits are listed in the relay guide. Nothing is charged to a company.

Where is our data?

On your family computer and on your phones. The relay holds only encrypted copies, so that phones can sync.

Does it work offline or away from home?

Yes. Phones keep working without a connection, and changes sync when they reach the relay again. Mobile data works too.

Do we need to be technical?

No programming is needed. Setup is a few guided steps: a free Cloudflare account, starting the app on the computer, and scanning a code on each phone.

What if a phone is lost?

If you can reach that phone, turn the relay off on it from Settings. Removing one lost phone for good isn't built yet, and we say so plainly.

Do iPhones get notifications?

Yes, from the Home Screen app. Notifications only say that something changed, and tapping one opens the app.

Does it work on Android?

Yes, in Chrome. Scan the code on the family computer, and the phone joins the family.

What's the catch?

The catch is the setup. You run it yourself, so you need a computer that stays on at home and a free Cloudflare account. We would rather say that plainly than hide it behind a subscription.

Does Cloudflare see our data?

Cloudflare hosts your relay on your own account, so it stores scrambled copies and sees when devices connect. It can't read the content, because the key lives on your family's devices and in the family link.

Do you track us?

No. The product page and the app use no analytics or trackers, and the app's font is bundled rather than fetched from Google. Three things reach outside services: push notifications pass through the browser's push service as an encrypted ping, looking up a book by ISBN asks Open Library for its details and cover, and the Mac app's daily check asks GitHub whether a newer version exists. You can turn that check off in the menu bar.

Why isn't there a cloud version?

A cloud version would put your family's data back on someone else's servers, which is the thing Yggltree exists to avoid. A hub on a cloud account you control is on the roadmap, for families without an always-on computer.
